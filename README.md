This project, titled "I cannot read Chinese", is a commentary on how broadly speaking, a lot of Singaporean youths are not able to speak their Mothertongue fluently or at a conversational level. Not only are they not able to speak the language, but most also brag about their inability to speak it, as a self-deprecating or just in a ironic way. Language is a cornerstone of culture, and being unable to speak the language of a culture, the Chinese language for myself, feels like I'm distanced from my identity and my cultural roots. It's ultimately a shame, which inspired me to create this project. Here, I hope for Singaporean Chinese youths like myself to reflect as they see their reflection behind the wall of difficult-to-read Chinese text, and as they lift their finger to hover over the Chinese text, it translates into English, though, the English is broken and borderline unreadbable, be it due to the differing sentence or grammatical structure of English versus Chinese, or simply because after attempting to translate the Chinese myself, I either reach for the simplest definition of the word I could manage, or simply was unable to translate it at all. 

I used p5.js as it is a familiar tool that I knew how to invoke a webcam out of, and would also be a good base to put together my poster. p5.js also allows me to input audio as well as visuals, so I could add quintessential Chinese elements like the guqin audio and the bright red chrysanthemums. 

One key challenge would be the method of portraying this project. I was initially unsure how to go about with the translation, as I had written out the text before considering that. I tried using a scratch card kind of effect where the mouse or a viewer's finger could "scratch" one layer off to reveal another layer of, but I felt like it would be ugly. Additionally, another challenge I faced was the gapping between the Chinese words when untranslated. Initally, to compensate for the English words, there were huge disproportionate gaps between the Chinese words in anticipation for when a finger hovered over to translate the one or two chinese characters into a long English word. To me, it felt clunky and more importantly, not like a graphic poster, so I opted to control the spacing between the Chinese words. However, that resulted in some difficulty reading the sentences in its entirety, as due to the length of the English word, it would be hard to read everything smoothly in one line. If possible, I would like to have changed this, though I am also largely unsure how to. 

Instructions to compile and run: download the project folder and unzip the folder, open the folder in visual studio code or upload to p5.js, start the sketch and allow for camera access.
